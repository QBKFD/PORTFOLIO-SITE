OPARLI: a falsification-first research system

A research system for commodities, validated on gold so far. Every claim goes through the same path: a pre-registration committed before any code touches an outcome, development data, one look at a holdout, then a single forward test on data that is sealed in code. Everything from October 2026 on can't be read until that final test.

What runs live is deliberately simple: a deterministic level scanner on an EC2 host, connected to IB Gateway, that sends a Telegram alert on the first touch of 12 level types and journals my take-or-skip reply. Nothing trades. Behind it sit a point-in-time datafeed (IBKR to Redis to SQLite), the HAR volatility model that won its study, and a LangGraph rebuild of the agents: visual, technical, sentiment and GEX analysts vote blind, then discuss, and a meta rule I set decides. The agents are built for shadow decisions only and are not deployed yet.

Quant hackathon: a bot-only competition platform

The platform behind a quant hackathon. Teams write bots and submit code, the platform runs every match in a sandbox, and the website shows leaderboards and replays. Nobody plays by hand. There are two games: Kelly Survival, a bet-sizing game where each bot is scored against an oracle over many secret paths, and Reconnaissance Blind Chess, chess where you can't see your opponent's pieces, played against house bots and against other teams.

Done so far: both game engines, pure and seeded, so the same seed and the same bots give a byte-identical replay; an out-of-process bot protocol with one validation boundary per game, so a late or crashing bot can't break a match; the participant kits for both games; a replay viewer; and house bots that were red-teamed before the event. Rankings are never decided by one game or one path. Next are the sandbox checks on Linux and the API and leaderboard.

The OPARLI ledger: what survived pre-registration

Studies on gold and silver, run in order, each with its plan committed before any result and a report whose first line is the verdict. Most came back null, and they stay in the ledger. Price levels behaved no differently from matched placebo levels (0 of 6 hypotheses supported), and the confirmation on then-sealed 2024–2026 data found the opposite of the predicted effect. Jump-model regimes lost to simple volatility terciles. Runs of small candles added nothing.

Three things held up. A HAR volatility model beats the plug-in estimate by 16 to 18% on QLIKE, in 24 of 24 folds. Telling it that a CPI, payrolls or FOMC release falls in the window cuts its error by another 18.6% at four hours. And breaks of the last swing high or low follow through less often than on a random walk: found on gold, replicated on silver, though fading them doesn't pay even before costs. A clean null is worth more than a backtest that was allowed to cheat.

Pre-registered · Sealed from Oct 2026, enforced in code · Holdout read once · BH-corrected

Can neural networks forecast commodities? Mostly, no

MSc thesis · reworked after submission

My master's thesis asked whether recurrent networks, attention hybrids and a Transformer can forecast next-day returns of gold, silver and copper from market and macro features. After submitting, I went back and fixed what made the results look better or worse than they were: macro data used before its release date, a target shifted by a day, scalers fitted on the test years. Then I added the naive baselines the thesis lacked. The honest answer: over 2020–2024, none of the 24 model and metal combinations beats a "no change" forecast.

Macro surprises do move metals, a one standard deviation upside surprise in US payrolls takes 39 bp off gold, but the move is over by the next day. What these inputs predict is how much prices move, not which way: a HAR model beats the historical average on volatility for all three metals, and no network beats HAR.

Deposit pass-through under stress: NL and BE savings, 2022–2026

Personal project · public ECB data only

Banks price non-maturing deposits with pass-through models, usually an error correction model fitted on a long history. That history was mostly falling rates and the zero floor. So I estimated the standard model family on data ending in 2021 and forecast Dutch and Belgian savings rates through the 2022 hikes and the 2024 cuts, with no refitting and every month built on the model's own previous prediction. The training window mattered more than the model: extending it from 2013 to 2021 cut the Dutch linear model's error by about two thirds. The cointegration the model assumes never shows up at 5%. Every specification beats "rates never move", but none significantly beats a one-number rule, and the slow models flip the sign of their error between the two phases.

XAI under pressure: what rule-based models actually cost

BSc thesis · University of Amsterdam

The starting point: RuleOpt, an optimization-based rule learning method co-developed by my ML professor and published shortly before, deserved an independent benchmark. I tested it alongside three other rule-based methods against standard black-box baselines across eleven classification datasets. On binary and low-complexity tasks, interpretable models matched the baselines within one to two points, in one case beating all of them with a rule set readable in a minute. On complex multiclass tasks, the gap widened past 15 points. Of nine published XAI methods evaluated, only four ran reliably: the tooling lags the literature. Full comparison in the repo.

Earnings-call sentiment: where the signal actually lives

FinBERT sentiment extraction across 18,755 earnings call transcripts, asking which part of the call actually carries financial signal. The finding: prepared remarks hold measurably stronger sentiment than the Q&A sections, which skew neutral, suggesting the scripted part of the call is where management commits to a tone and the spontaneous part hedges. A practical engineering result along the way: removing stop-words made processing three times faster but weakened the sentiment signal, a trade-off worth knowing before preprocessing financial text. Full methodology and the stock-impact extension in the repo.
